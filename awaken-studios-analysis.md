## 2. The script format

### Hard specs (measured)
- **Length:** 7:00–8:15. **Word count:** 1,100–1,300. **Pace:** about 150–162 words per minute, measured from captions on every long video (the captions include some [music] tags, so this is approximate).
- **Voices:** one narrator in the second person ("you"), plus short lines from on-screen characters. In the captions, `>>` marks a change of speaker, and these come every 20–40 seconds.
- **No** sponsor read, no "subscribe" request, and no "hey guys" intro. The first word is already the story.

### The beat sheet (consistent across the "What if" videos)

| Time | Beat | Real lines |
|---|---|---|
| **0:00–0:10** | **A normal moment with a specific detail, then the world breaks.** Second person, present tense, an exact time or day. | "You wake up on a Monday and something is wrong. No traffic, no sirens, no neighbor drilling into the wall at 7:00 a.m." / "At 9:41, every man on Earth disappears." / "Tuesday night, 9:40 p.m. … Your mom is calling." |
| **0:10–0:30** | **"Except you"**, then the stakes in one sentence, then a short comedy line. | "Well, almost every person." / "the entire future of the human species now rests on two strangers who met about 40 seconds ago." / "What could possibly go wrong? Let's see if you keep that energy." |
| **~0:25–0:35** | **The title appears on screen, word for word.** Big title card, music hit. Some videos add a list of what's coming: "Stick around and we'll squeeze all of us into a space the size of Delaware, plant a farm bigger than two continents…" | Title card: "WHAT IF YOU WERE THE LAST MAN ON EARTH?" |
| **0:35–2:00** | **Act 1: consequences.** Rapid stats, each tied to a picture you can imagine. Usually framed as "the easy version" first. | "Power line workers? About 96% men. So the grid starts failing… Truck drivers? Over 90% men… food shortages in about 3 days." |
| **~1:30–2:30** | **The turn.** "Easy… except." | "Keep going and you pass 8 billion in about 16 generations… Easy. Except there's a problem with generation two." / "But there's one problem nobody can fix. No men means no babies." |
| **2:30–5:00** | **Act 2: the story plays out.** Each new scene is a fresh complication: comedy set-pieces (chase, disguise, dating show) alternating with hard science (genetics, IVF, egg counts). Each section ends on a joke or a twist. | Disguise "Brenda" / "Steve", "The Last Bachelorette… you'd finish season one in about 76 million years." |
| **Throughout** | **Historical case studies as proof.** One real-world example per act. | Habsburgs, Old Blue the robin, Kowloon Walled City, a Moroccan sultan with ~1,000 kids, Genghis Khan's Y chromosome, Mitochondrial Eve, WWII women workers, Chernobyl wolves |
| **4:00–6:30** | **The time-skip montage.** Time jumps forward with on-screen year counters. | "Year one, a crib. Year five, a bunk bed. Year 12, a house so loud you start sleeping in the car. 20 years in…" / "Year 13… 18 years in… 50 years later…" |
| **~4:00–6:00** | **The emotional low point.** One sincere, dark beat. | "you wonder whether the two of you gave them a future or just a very slow ending." / male suicide stat / Mom's voicemail: "Hey, it's Mom. Leave me a message." |
| **Last 60s** | **Answer the title directly**: "So, could X? [two-word answer]. [nuance]." | "So, could two people actually repopulate Earth? Technically, maybe." / "Alone? No… But one woman with a whole planet behind her? Yes." |
| **Last 10s** | **Punchline, then a question for the comments.** | "You should have made more friends." → "who would you want standing next to you on that empty street?" / "mostly from a deck chair." / "Last man or last woman? Which would you rather be? Tell me in the comments." |

### Writing rules they follow
1. **Every number gets a comparison.** 1M tons of poop a day becomes "three Empire State Buildings of poop". 1,280 ancestors becomes "a group that could fit inside a high school gym". The city becomes "a bit bigger than Texas… almost 200 Jakartas".
2. **A stat every 10–15 seconds, a joke every 20–30 seconds**, alternating so neither runs long.
3. **Named side characters with callbacks:** Gus the pigeon, Brenda the mannequin, Steve the disguise, Gary with the tomato, Kevin the bear bodyguard, Linda "Head of Overthinking", DJ Dopamine, "Big Pro" (prolactin), Miss Oxy (oxytocin), Pickles the cat. The ones that come back (Linda, Gus, Mom's voicemail) tie the video together.
4. **Short fragments, often in threes:** "No traffic, no sirens, no neighbor…" / "Suit, rose, hair. Close enough." / "Wombs. Eggs. IVF."
5. **Hedging builds trust:** "Reportedly. Nobody has ever been able to check." / "Not every scientist accepts that estimate, but…" Each description lists real papers (e.g. Hu et al. 2023 *Science*, Alvarez et al. 2009 *PLoS ONE*) and links an "Awaken Corrections Desk" form.
6. **Innuendo, never explicit:** "let's call them appointments", "we can't say it on YouTube, so let's call it clarity mode", "Fill a cup and go back to the pool." This keeps sexual curiosity safe for advertisers.
7. **Make internet culture part of the plot:** "man or bear" becomes a story beat (she picks the bear and it becomes her bodyguard), along with ring-light influencers and reaction videos.
8. **Turn a topic into characters (orgasm video):** the brain becomes a "mission control" crew, hormones become characters, chapters are numbered title cards ("02 THE WIRING", "03 THE RUSH", "04 THE AFTER").

### Shorts format (30–60 seconds, about 140–230 words)
The first line drops the viewer into the middle of the action: "The guard reached into the cell, and the prisoner's head rolled onto the floor. It was fake." Then the story is told fast, with **no resolution**: the Alcatraz Short cuts off mid-sentence ("But on Alcatraz, the next morning,") and the Twin Towers Short ends "All because, in 1974,". That makes the video loop back to the start. The "2 People" Short is the full video compressed to 63 seconds and ends on the same comment question.

## 3. The visual style

### Character system (the brand)
- **"Bean" people:** perfectly round, solid-black heads and simple black limbs, with **two large white oval eyes and no other facial features**, except for an occasional mouth or blush in close-ups. Gender and identity come entirely from **hair, clothing colour and accessories** (gold hoops, ties, beanies).
- **Emotion through cartoon symbols:** worried eyebrows, a blue sweat drop, heart eyes, sly narrowed eyes, pink blush. These read clearly even at thumbnail size and need almost no animation effort.
- The characters stay the same across all videos, so a viewer can recognise an Awaken video before seeing the channel name.

### How the look has changed
| Video | Rendering |
|---|---|
| Video 1 (2 People) | **Flat 2D vector.** Muted dusk palette (lavender, navy, warm amber street-light glow). Most of it is a chalkboard explainer (family-tree math, glowing gene "pairs" on navy, portrait gallery for the Habsburgs). Simple and cheap to make. |
| Videos 2–4 (City, Last Guy, Last Girl) | **3D-style diorama sets.** Toon-shaded, slightly low-poly city streets, apartments and forests with warm lighting and soft depth of field. The flat characters are placed inside these 3D scenes. Lots of globes, maps and city-scale wide shots. |
| Video 5 (Orgasm) | **3D neon "control room"** sets in hot pink and teal, inside-the-body scenes, nightclub lighting, title cards between chapters. Clearly the most expensive production so far. |

Takeaway: they reached product-market fit with cheap 2D, then put the money from the hit into better production.

### On-screen graphics they use repeatedly
- **Big running counters:** "8,599,999,999", "4,100,000,000 → 1" (women on Earth), "308,847,550".
- **Night-side Earth globes**, then zooms down to a map (Delaware, Italy, Africa, Mississippi).
- **Scale comparisons:** a water cube next to a skyline, a trash pile beside Empire State Buildings, a balance scale.
- **Stat chyrons** as lower-thirds: "96% of line workers are men", "1 in 20 pilots is a woman".
- **Year badges** in the top-right corner during time-skips ("YEAR 5", "+70 YEARS", "21 YEARS").
- **In-world text gags:** news tickers, protest signs ("BEST DAY EVER!!"), shopfronts ("WIG OUT!", "SUDS & SPIN", "TONY'S PIZZA"), a "SOURCE: COW" sticker on a textbook.
- **Pacing:** nearly every storyboard frame (roughly one every 4–5 seconds) shows a different shot, so cuts come every few seconds and rarely stay on one composition longer than about 5 seconds.

## 4. The thumbnail formula

| Video | Thumbnail text | Image |
|---|---|---|
| 2 People (5.3M) | **ONLY 2 LEFT** ("2" in yellow) | Two worried bean characters alone on an empty dusk street, lit by a streetlamp |
| Last Guy (755K) | **4B WOMEN?!** ("WOMEN?!" in yellow) | Sweating guy in a suit pulling at his collar. A queue of heart-eyed women behind him and one lying seductively in the foreground |
| Last Girl (767K) | **ONLY GIRL!** ("GIRL!" in yellow) | Nervous girl in the centre, surrounded by grinning men rubbing their hands together |
| 8B in One City (27K) | **8 BILLION PEOPLE** | Crowded subway car, one worried face in the middle |
| His vs Hers (18K) | **HIS vs HERS** | Split screen, blue vs pink, two blushing faces |

**The rules:**
1. **2–3 words max, never the same as the title.** The title asks the question. The thumbnail gives the twist or the number ("Can 2 People Really Restart Humanity?" + "ONLY 2 LEFT").
2. **A number or extreme quantity** in almost every one (2, 4B, 8 BILLION).
3. **Two-tone text:** cream-white plus **one word in yellow**, in a very heavy rounded display font with a soft shadow, placed top-left or top-centre.
4. **One anxious main character against a crowd that wants something from them.** The tension comes from the main character's discomfort (sweat drop, raised eyebrows) set against everyone else's desire (heart eyes, sly grins). The two big gender videos are mirror images of each other.
5. **Saturated blue or purple backgrounds with a warm glow or rim light** behind the main character, so the figure separates from the background at small sizes.
6. **The thumbnails are more polished than the videos:** glossier, more detailed illustration (fabric folds, hair strands, glow). The thumbnails are illustrated poster art, not frames taken from the video. Their rendering looks a lot like modern AI image-generation output, but I can't confirm the tool.
7. **The weaker thumbnails** (city and His vs Hers) lack rule 4. Nobody is in a socially charged predicament.

## 5. The title formula

**Pattern:** `[Can / Could / What if] + [You / 2 People / 8 Billion People] + [extreme situation] + ?`
- 5–8 words, about 35–40 characters, so they fit untruncated on mobile.
- Always contains **"you" or a number**.
- One intensifier: "**Really**", "**ONE**" (in caps), "**Last**".
- Casual words in titles ("Guy", "Girl"), formal words in tags ("last man on earth", "last woman").
- Paired titles mirror each other exactly: "What if You're The Last Guy…" / "What if You Were The Last Girl…".
- The one title that broke the pattern ("Male Orgasm vs Female Orgasm: The Science Behind It") is a keyword-style title with no "you" and no hypothetical. It is also the weakest by views per day.
- **Shorts** use story-hook titles ("The Man Who Walked Across the Twin Towers", "The Only Men Who Ever Escaped Alcatraz?"). The Twin Towers title has a "the The" typo that they never fixed.

**Metadata pattern:** 15–20 tags mixing the exact search phrase ("could 2 people repopulate earth"), the specific facts covered ("Habsburg jaw", "black robin Old Blue"), and format tags ("what if animation", "science animation"). Descriptions are a 2-line hook, then chapters, then a summary paragraph full of keywords, then a **source list**, then a comment question, then 3 hashtags, then the corrections link.

## 6. Why it works: the formula

1. **A universal hypothetical with you at the centre.** You don't need to know anything to start watching, and "you" + "except you" makes the viewer the main character. This is the classic *What If* genre, aimed at as broad an audience as possible. The 0.63% like rate shows the algorithm served it far beyond any core audience.
2. **Curiosity about sex and reproduction, made safe with science.** Every hit is quietly about who has babies with whom. Viewers are drawn to the forbidden topic, and the genetics framing keeps it advertiser-safe and shareable. When the topic became openly sexual (the orgasm video), reach dropped.
3. **A story, not a lecture.** The genre usually means a narrator over infographics. Awaken writes a **plot** (chase, disguise, kidnapping by a general, bear bodyguard) and puts the facts into it. Open story questions keep people watching ("then what happens in year 20?"), and the time-skip structure always hints at a payoff further ahead.
4. **Ups and downs keep attention:** an easy, optimistic version, then "except", then a joke, then a dark beat, then a hopeful case study, then a twist. No section stays in one mood longer than about 30 seconds.
5. **Credibility signals that big science channels use:** cited papers, open hedging, a public corrections desk. These earn trust, and they also give commenters something to argue about.
6. **Distinctive, cheap characters:** the faceless bean people are easy to animate, readable as thumbnails, and recognisable across videos.
7. **Doubling down on the winner:** when one premise hits, they make immediate mirrored sequels (Guy/Girl), link each in the other's description and closing line ("Meanwhile, in another video, a guy in a sundress has the opposite problem"), and finish with a comment debate ("Last man or last woman?"). Each video sends viewers to the next.
8. **Shorts as trailers plus high frequency:** a long video every 3 days, and Shorts that either compress the hit or loop history cliffhangers.

## 7. Template to use this formula on your own channel

Use your own character design and topics. Copying their bean characters or titles one-for-one would read as a clone. The formula is the part worth taking.

```
TITLE:     What if / Could [you | N people] [extreme reversal of normal life]?   (≤40 chars)
THUMBNAIL: 2–3 words, number + yellow keyword, anxious hero vs. a crowd that wants something
LENGTH:    7:00–8:00, ~1,150–1,250 words (~155 wpm)

0:00  Normal moment with an exact time ("Tuesday, 9:40 p.m."), then the world breaks. "Except you."
0:15  Stakes in one sentence plus a joke.
0:30  Title card (title repeated exactly). Optional "stick around and we'll…" list.
0:35  ACT 1  Consequences: 5–6 stats, each with a picture you can imagine.
1:45  THE TURN  "Easy. Except…"
2:00  ACT 2  3–4 scenes: comedy set-piece, science, historical case study, repeat.
              Name 2–3 side characters. Bring one back later.
4:30  TIME-SKIP MONTAGE  "Year 1… Year 5… 20 years in…" with on-screen year badges
5:30  EMOTIONAL LOW  One sincere beat (loss, loneliness, a hard stat)
6:00  HOPE  The real-world precedent that shows it could work
6:45  ANSWER  "So, could X? [2 words]. [one-line nuance]."
7:15  PUNCHLINE, then a comment question. Link the companion video.
DESCRIPTION: hook → chapters → summary → sources → question → 3 hashtags → corrections link
NEXT VIDEO:  if one hits, make the mirrored version within a week
```
