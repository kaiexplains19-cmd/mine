# Arc Lab: Series Bible

The fixed format of the show. The skill reads this on every run. Edit it to change the show; `TODO` fields get filled in on the first run.

## Identity

- **Show:** Arc Lab
- **Channel handle:** TODO
- **Promise:** One real experiment per episode. See it happen, then understand why.
- **Audience:** Curious 14-35 year olds who like seeing things happen more than reading about them. They know school science but not the math.
- **Niche terms (for topic research):** science experiments, physics explained, electricity experiment, chemistry reaction, "what happens if", slow motion science

## Voice

- **Narrator:** TODO (default: one calm, curious narrator voice, the same TTS voice every episode)
- Second person and present tense ("watch the needle", "you'd expect...").
- Make a prediction before every reveal, then let the result confirm or break it.
- No jargon without a picture. Every new term gets an on-screen lower-third the first time it's said.
- Don't hype. The experiment is the wow, not the adjectives.

## Episode structure (main format, 6-9 min)

1. **Cold open (0-15 s):** the most surprising moment of the experiment, then the question it raises.
2. **Title card (2 s):** the Arc Lab logo sting.
3. **Setup:** what we're testing and what you'd expect. Ask the viewer to commit to a guess.
4. **Run 1:** the experiment and the first result.
5. **Why it happens:** the explanation, built up visually in 2-3 steps.
6. **Twist / Run 2:** change one variable. The result should surprise again.
7. **Real world:** where this shows up outside the lab.
8. **Recap (15 s)** and **next-episode tease.**
9. **End card (10 s).**

Short format (`--short`, 45-60 s, 9:16): cold open, run, one-line why, twist, loop back to the first frame.

## Packaging rules

- Title: a question or a "what happens if" built around one concrete object, 60 characters or fewer, no clickbait the episode doesn't pay off.
- Thumbnail: one object mid-reaction, at most 3 words, and the arc-blue accent somewhere in frame.
- Name episodes `Arc Lab #NN: <title>` in the playlist only, not in the YouTube title.

## Safety rules (non-negotiable)

- Never give build steps, parts lists or settings for high-voltage devices (Tesla coils, Jacob's ladders, microwave transformers, stun guns), explosives, or toxic or reactive chemical preparations. Show the phenomenon and explain it, but don't teach someone to make it.
- Any experiment a viewer could copy gets the amber banner, "Don't try this at home" or a specific precaution, on screen when it first appears, plus a note in the description.
- Safe home experiments (static electricity, oobleck, lemon batteries and the like) are encouraged. Put their steps in the description.
- No health or medical claims without a source.

## Accuracy rules

- Every factual claim needs a source: a textbook, a peer-reviewed paper or a university or agency page. Sources go in the description.
- When a simplification bends the truth, say so in one line ("it's a bit more complicated than that, but...").
